\begin{abstract}
This report examines how MicroMacro approximation error scales with the
recovery rate $\gamma$ in an SIR epidemic model.
All scenarios share $\beta = 0.003$, chain-of-cliques topology
($L=10$, $n=100$, $d=6$, $k=5$), and 100 paired replications.
The error $\DRMS$ (MicroMacro $-$ full Gillespie) is significantly negative
across all scenarios and grows monotonically with $\gamma$.
Recovery exacerbates premature hazard export because the macro layer
does not account for wave passage: boundary nodes may have already
recovered by the time the exported macro hazard fires.
\textit{Ukrainian summary}: see \texttt{docs/ce04\_sir\_validation/report.md}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Objective}
Determine whether adding SIR recovery ($\gamma > 0$) to the SI baseline
amplifies or reduces MicroMacro approximation error.

\subsection{Network and Parameters}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Topology: chain-of-cliques, $L=10$ communities, $n=100$ nodes each,
        degree $d=6$, $k=5$ inter-community edges.
  \item Infection rate: $\beta = 0.003$ (within and between communities).
  \item Recovery rates: $\gamma \in \{0,\; 0.0005,\; 0.001,\; 0.003\}$.
  \item Basic reproduction number: $R_0 = \beta/\gamma \in \{\infty, 6, 3, 1\}$.
  \item Replications: 100 paired runs (same seed for Micro and MicroMacro).
  \item Horizon: $T = 5000$ time units; epidemic declared complete
        when no infected nodes remain or $t > T$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Primary Metric}
\[
  \DRMS = \overline{T_{\text{MM}}} - \overline{T_{\text{Micro}}}
\]
where $T$ denotes mean first arrival time at the last community.
A negative $\DRMS$ indicates premature arrival under MicroMacro.
Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals use 10{,}000 resamples.

\textbf{Critical observation:} At $\gamma = 0.003$ ($R_0 = 1$), only 28\% of full
Gillespie replications produce a chain-wide epidemic, but MicroMacro
declares completion in 72\% of replications --- a \textbf{false-positive rate
of 44 percentage points}.  This is a qualitatively different failure mode:
MicroMacro does not merely predict early arrival, it predicts \emph{arrival
when no arrival would have occurred} in the stochastic reality.

\section{Interpretation}

\subsection{Mechanism of Error Amplification}

Under the two-scale approximation, the macro layer fires an import event
using the aggregate fraction $\bar{i}(t)$ of infected nodes at the moment
of export.  In an SIR model, $\bar{i}(t)$ first rises (infection wave) and
then falls (recovery).  There are two failure modes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Wave-passage mismatch.}  After the peak, $\bar{i}(t)$ is
        falling. If a macro transition fires when the wave has already passed,
        the hazard is smaller than it was at the wave's peak, yet the event
        fires because the aggregate macro rate is integrated over the full
        wave shape, not the current one.  Result: artificially early arrivals.

  \item \textbf{Censoring effect.}  With high $\gamma$, many replications
        reach $R_0 \approx 1$ and fail to produce a full chain-wide epidemic.
        These censored runs (no arrival) are absent from the MicroMacro
        distribution but inflate the Micro mean --- or are absent from both,
        depending on implementation.  The \emph{conditional} mean under
        MicroMacro is then far more negative.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Significance for the Paper}

The SI case is the paper's primary scenario.  The CE-04 results show:
\begin{itemize}
  \item MicroMacro is premature even in the SI baseline ($\DRMS \approx -1068$).
  \item Recovery ($\gamma > 0$) monotonically \emph{worsens} this error.
  \item The paper's claim about premature hazard export is thus conservative:
        the real-world SIR error is larger than the SI error reported.
  \item Near $R_0 = 1$ ($\gamma = 0.003$), MicroMacro generates a
        44-percentage-point false-positive rate for epidemic completion.
        This is a qualitatively distinct failure: the model predicts
        epidemics that would \emph{never occur} in a stochastic simulation.
  \item The rel-$W_1$ distance is non-monotone at $\gamma = 0.003$
        (drops from 0.4703 to 0.4399) because most censored Micro runs
        have no arrival time, compressing the reference distribution.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Recovery amplifies MicroMacro approximation error in two distinct ways.
First, the conditional $\DRMS$ grows monotonically: from $-1068$ (SI) to
$-1726$ (SIR, $\gamma=0.003$), a 62\% increase.
Second, and more critically, near the epidemic threshold ($R_0=1$),
MicroMacro generates 44 percentage points more false epidemic completions
than the full stochastic model.
Both failure modes trace to the wave-passage mismatch: the aggregate
macro hazard does not account for the post-peak decline of $\bar{i}(t)$
nor for the probability that individual boundary nodes have already recovered.
Future work should develop bridge-aware hazard corrections that condition
on gateway-node infection state, not on the aggregate community fraction.
