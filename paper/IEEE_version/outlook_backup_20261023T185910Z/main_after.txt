    ABSTRACT Stochastic epidemic simulation often lacks information about individual contacts. Aggregate
    data on movements between communities may nevertheless be available. Internal contact networks can
    be approximately reconstructed from structural characteristics, while specific inter-community contacts
    remain unknown. We study a two-scale stochastic simulation method (TwoScale) that combines detailed
    local Gillespie simulators with aggregate inter-community transmission rates, without explicitly represent-
    ing inter-community contacts. We prove that the ideal two-scale process is equivalent to exact simulation
    on a surrogate network with uniform inter-community connections. We identify the mechanisms of dis-
    crepancy from the full-network model and establish that the first infection export is stochastically no later
    than in the full-network model under uniformly random bridge-node placement in the theoretical setting
    considered. We assess the method’s accuracy by comparing it with full-network Gillespie simulation on
    synthetic SI networks, including 48 configurations of chains of ten communities. The experiments showed
    close time distributions when infection spread rapidly within communities: in a chain of ten cliques, the
    normalised Wasserstein distance after nine inter-community crossings was 3.34%. However, discrepancies
    increased substantially in communities with longer internal paths and lower connectivity, reaching 40.9%
    in the most discrepant configuration. Their magnitude also depended on community size and the number
    of inter-community edges. The proposed time-scale measure showed a strong rank association with the
    magnitude of the discrepancies (Spearman correlation 0.91). These results help identify conditions under
    which aggregate connections reproduce temporal characteristics close to full-network simulation without
    specifying individual inter-community contacts.
